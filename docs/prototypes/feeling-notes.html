Notes on a Feeling

A few words of your own against a single word — only about the meeting, not about him — written in a drawer the screen makes room for, and gone again the moment you clear the box. Shown in the grid and in the walk. Everything below is live: click into a demo and use the keys.

The drawer

The screen parks and makes room

The picker pushes itself up, fades, and dissolves into the top edge; a drawer comes in off the bottom carrying the word and a box that starts one line tall and grows to five. The modal's own height never changes, so nothing behind it moves — and because the drawer grows upwards, the box stays where the thumbs and the software keyboard already are.

While the picker has focus
NWrite a note on this word, or open the one already there. Focus moves into the box.
EnterKeep it. In the grid it marks the word and hands focus back to it; in the walk it answers yes and deals the next card.
Shift EnterA new line. The box grows under it, and the drawer climbs to make room.
Enter on emptyDeletes the note. Clearing the box is the delete; there is no second control for it.
EscPuts the box back the way it was found and closes the drawer. Escape never leaves the screen while a note is open.
← →In the walk they answer — except inside the box, where they are the caret.

A note only exists on a word that is picked. There is nothing a note on an unpicked feeling could mean and nowhere for it to be written. So the rule runs both ways: keeping a note marks the word, unmarking a word takes its note with it, and in the walk — where every card is answered — keeping a note is the yes.

The open question

How anyone finds out it is there

N is invisible, and on a phone there is no keyboard to press it with. Whatever teaches it has to live in the one reserved line under the definition — the line the note itself comes back on — because that is the only place on either screen that is about one word. Four candidates, switched live; every demo on this page follows the choice.

The invitation, and only on a marked word. It is the one candidate that is both a target and a lesson: it sits exactly where the note will sit, wearing the same shape, so what teaches the feature is a preview of its result — and the keycap on the right teaches the shortcut the way a menu does. Showing it only once a word is marked ties it to the rule that makes a note legal, so marking a word is what introduces notes, and sweeping the pointer across twenty-eight unmarked words offers nothing.

In the walk the invitation is always there, because every card can be noted: keeping a note is the same yes the → gives.

Reading them against each other

What each way of offering it costs

Teaches the keyWorks by touchWhat it costs
The invitation Yes — the keycap sits on the line, the way a menu shows its shortcut. Yes. The whole line is the target, and it is the width of the modal. A line of quiet type under every marked word. The loudest of the four, which is the point.
The pencil No. It says a note is possible, never which key opens one. Yes, but it is a small target at the end of a line. Almost nothing, and it reads as chrome rather than as an offer.

Nothing but the key No. No — which rules it out on its own, phone being the surface this is for. Nothing, and nobody finds it.

Ship the invitation; keep the pencil for the walk if the card gets crowded. The two are the same control at two volumes, so the choice can be made per screen without anything else changing — and both leave the same trace when a note is written, which is the badge below.

Which words carry one

A badge that says there is writing here

A note is invisible until you land on the word, which is no good in a grid of twenty-eight or in a category card that lists what you picked. Three ways to say it, switched live.

The icon is the one worth the width. The dot and the rule are free — paint inside padding the pill already has — but both read as a rendering artefact until someone tells you what they mean. The glyph says writing on its own, and because a noted word is always a marked one, the badge never appears on a word that was not already drawn differently: a row can only grow when you add a note to it, by about one character, and never under the finger that is tapping something else.

What it touches

Where a note has to live

A note is picked state, so it belongs beside visited in the two picker machines and has to survive the round trip through the fence in the note — which is the real design question here, not the keystrokes. Now that a note runs to several lines, it cannot ride in a parenthesis on the category bullet. 

- Angry: irate, livid
    - irate: only about the meeting, not about him.
      It ran an hour over and I never got to speak.

An indented bullet under the category, keyed by the word, with wrapped lines indented past it. The block still reads with the plugin off, the category bullets are untouched — so resolve.ts keeps working out the inventory from the words alone — and a note may contain any character at all, which the parenthesis form could not offer. The cost is that the parser gains a second kind of line, and a note whose word is not in the bullet above it is one more way for a block to fail to read.
